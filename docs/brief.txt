
WEPs Portal Mockup Design and Functional Instructions
Developer handoff for the UN Women Women’s Empowerment Principles portal
Prepared by Potential.com | 19 October 2026 | Version 1.0
Purpose of this document
Build a polished, clickable WEPs portal mockup that shows how companies can apply, receive approval, onboard their teams, learn, obtain expert support, participate in community activities, purchase proposed certifications, and submit annual reports through one connected experience.
This brief gives designers and developers the context, page content, permissions, states, sample scenarios and interaction rules needed to build the mockup without prior knowledge of UN Women, WEPs or the opportunity.
What the first delivery must demonstrate
An attractive public website and modern company application with an administrator review loop.
Five stakeholder roles with distinct dashboards and shared company records.
Department-based learning, points that unlock clinics, and points redeemed for human mentorship.
Annual reporting with history, community discussions and moderated company product and service listings.
One UN Women workspace with clearly separated Reporting and Platform Management sections.
Build an experience prototype with coherent sample data and working transitions. Use simulated invitations, AI responses, payments, calendars and downloads where live services are not configured. Label demo entry and payment states clearly.
Important product distinction
Company application requirements and the official WEPs framework are source-based. Portal layouts, gamification, marketplace flows, AI features and the three paid certification concepts are proposed features requested for this mockup. Do not present them as existing or endorsed WEPs services.

1 Background and product objectives
UN Women works on gender equality and women’s empowerment. WEPs is a framework established by UN Women and the UN Global Compact that helps businesses advance these goals across their workplaces, markets and communities. Companies join as organisations; people participate through their company accounts. [S1]
Opportunity and existing pain points
In the 16 October discussion, the WEPs team described fragmented participation and limited capacity to connect learning, live support and results. Their follow-up document identifies separate touchpoints for assessments, surveys, learning, webinars, resources, action planning and reporting. It also describes limited visibility into engagement, needs and subsequent company action. [S5, S6]
The portal should connect those activities around one company record and linked employee profiles. UN Women should be able to understand a company’s needs, the support it used, its implementation progress and its annual submissions without assembling separate spreadsheets.
Official framework and proposed portal flow
The official WEPs journey has six stages: Consider, Sign, Activate, Engage, Sustain and Report. Keep these names when describing the official journey. They are different from a proposed portal workflow or course completion sequence. [S1]
Use principle tags for leadership; fair treatment; health and safety; education and advancement; enterprise and supply chains; community advocacy; and measurement and reporting. Link each tag to its official principle page. These are navigation themes, not invented certification levels. [S1]
Desired demonstration outcomes
Companies understand their next action, can delegate work and can find relevant support.
Employees receive learning suited to their department and see how preparation unlocks live participation.
Mentors and trainers manage the support they actually provide.
UN Women sees engagement, operational exceptions, submitted evidence and reporting progress in one place.
Potential supplies the modular platform foundation. The prototype illustrates a WEPs-specific configuration; deployment, integrations and final scope are agreed later. [S7]

Role

 | UN Women Global Administrator
 | All companies and users; applications; programmes; content; approvals; reporting; points rules; mentor and trainer management.
 | Company CEO / Executive Sponsor
 | Own company profile, leadership commitment, delegation, priorities, reporting approval, certification purchases and company listings.
 | Company Administrator / WEPs Focal Point
 | Delegated management of own company users, assessments, action plan, learning coordination, reports and listings.
 | Company Employee
 | Own learning, resources, eligible sessions, points, community participation and assigned contributions.
 | Mentor / Trainer
 | Own expertise profile and availability, assigned sessions, webinars or clinics, shared materials and relevant participation records.
 | Rules that apply everywhere
Companies self-apply. The global administrator reviews, approves or requests further information before portal activation. Administrators may also invite a company or CEO into the same application process.
A CEO can appoint an existing colleague or invite a new company administrator. Allow selectable permissions for profile editing, team management, assessment, action planning, reporting, purchasing and marketplace submissions.
The CEO can perform administrator functions from one account. Do not force duplicate accounts or a second onboarding journey.
Employees are attached to one company in this mockup and have a department. They do not see the company baseline assessment, billing or unpublished annual reports by default.
Mentors/trainers choose one-to-one, group sessions or both. Their dashboard adapts to those capabilities; this does not create extra stakeholder roles.
Company access, signatory status, certification status and points are separate attributes. Payment or activity points never confer signatory status.
Show permission-aware navigation and actions. Do not rely only on hiding menu items: direct access to an unauthorised prototype route must also show an access message or return the user to their dashboard.
3 Visual design and interaction system
Design direction
Create a modern institutional experience that feels welcoming, credible and easy to navigate. Use generous white space, strong photography, clear hierarchy, restrained colour and purposeful motion. Public pages can be more expressive; authenticated workspaces should prioritise clarity and completing tasks.
Follow the WEPs identity at weps.org. Before implementation, obtain approved WEPs and UN Women assets and verify their colours, typography, spacing and permitted logo combinations. Preserve supplied logo artwork and proportions. Do not redraw logos or treat an invented emblem as official.
Provisional design tokens for the mockup
Element
 | Instruction
 | Colour
 | Use white and pale neutral surfaces, deep navy text, blue primary actions, and small warm accent details. Suggested starting tokens: #153B55, #167CB3, #F4F7FA and #D6A44A. These are proposed UI colours, not verified official brand codes.
 | Typography
 | Use an approved brand font if supplied; otherwise use a clean sans serif. Suggested scale: hero 48 to 60 px, page title 30 to 36 px, section 22 to 26 px, body 16 px, labels 14 px.
 | Components
 | Use consistent 12 to 16 px card radii, subtle borders, light shadows, clear status badges, named icons and readable chart labels.
 | Layout
 | Design at 1440 px desktop with an approximately 1200 px public content width. Portal uses a 240 px sidebar, top utility bar and responsive main canvas.
 | Motion
 | Use 150 to 250 ms transitions, gentle hero entry and short progress celebrations. Respect reduced motion; avoid scroll hijacking and continuous distracting effects.
 | Usability and responsive behaviour
Provide keyboard navigation, visible focus, text labels for icons and accessible contrast. Never encode chart or status meaning through colour alone.
At mobile widths, collapse side navigation into a drawer; stack cards; keep key actions visible; provide a usable scroll or card alternative for wide tables.
Use skeleton loading, helpful empty states, inline field errors, confirmations for submitted actions and consistent back navigation.
Every dashboard should have an obvious next action. Avoid displaying every available metric above the fold.

7 Application review and sign in
Application workflow
Draft > Submitted > Under Review > Additional Information Requested > Resubmitted > Approved. Also provide a Not Approved outcome with a reason. Every transition appears in an activity timeline. Approval creates or activates the company workspace and grants signatory access according to the agreed WEPs process.
Administrator opens the application, reviews grouped fields and documents, and selects Approve or Request Information. Requests specify the exact missing item, message and optional due date.
The applicant receives a simulated email notification and sees the same request on the status screen. Previously submitted information remains saved. Resubmission preserves previous document versions and review notes.
Detect an existing company match and offer a reviewed claim/reactivation route. Do not create a second signatory automatically. Administrator-invited companies follow the same validation and review.
Sign in page
Use a split layout: brand image and short value statement on one side; email sign-in and invitation acceptance on the other. Include Forgot Password, Join WEPs, and a clearly labelled Explore Demo section.

8 Company profile team and delegation
Company profile
Use a large company header with logo, approved name, country, sector, signatory status and member-since field. Below it place tabs for Overview, Company Details, Team and Permissions, Action Plan, and Activity. Annual Reports is accessible from the main navigation and profile summary.
Overview displays approved company summary, executive commitment, priority principles, participation and report status.
Company Details allows authorised edits to company and contact information. Changes to identity or approved public information can show a review-pending state.
Keep the private workspace separate from the public company profile. Provide Preview Public Profile showing only approved information; private assessments, files and billing never appear there.
Team management
Show a searchable table with name, email, department, role, invitation status, last activity and learning progress. Provide Invite User, Resend Invitation, Edit Department, Assign Administrator and Remove Company Access actions. Removing access does not erase historical company reporting evidence.
Delegation panel
The CEO can select permissions individually: edit company profile, invite/manage employees, manage assessment and action plan, prepare annual reports, submit annual reports, manage marketplace listings and purchase certifications. Default a new company administrator to operational responsibilities; financial and final submission privileges are explicit toggles.
Show a confirmation summary before granting permissions. Preserve one account when a CEO also performs administration. Multiple company administrators may exist, with the CEO retaining visibility into who has which rights.
Department structure
Seed Human Resources, Procurement, Marketing and Communications, Sustainability and Reporting, Operations, and Leadership. Department drives learning recommendations; it is not an additional stakeholder role. Employees can request a correction rather than silently change the reporting structure.
Action plan
Include a practical action board under company management: action title, principle, baseline, target, owner, department, deadline, status and evidence. Company administrators create and assign; employees see only their assigned contributions. Link actions to learning, expert support and annual report sections.
9 Company CEO and administrator dashboards
Company CEO dashboard
Purpose: provide a concise executive view of company participation and decisions. Start with Welcome, company name and two actions: Review Company Progress and Manage Team.
Top cards: employees activated, company learning completion, actions on track and annual report status. Each opens its underlying records.
Company priorities: three approved priorities with accountable colleagues, deadlines and progress. Use a seven-principle summary for navigation, not an unexplained composite score.
Decisions required: approve an action plan, confirm an annual report, assign an administrator or approve a certification purchase.
Engagement and results: department participation chart, action completion trend and clearly labelled company-reported indicators with period and evidence status.
Upcoming support: relevant clinics, booked mentoring and recommended next support. Include certification and community shortcuts lower on the page.
When the CEO also acts as company administrator, expose operational tools through Manage Company. Keep one account and one company record.
Company administrator dashboard
Purpose: coordinate the team and keep the company moving. Start with an operational checklist and a prominent next action.
Top cards: invited versus active users, completed learning, upcoming eligible clinics, overdue company actions and annual reporting deadline.
Team readiness: departments with enrolled users, completed prerequisites and clinic eligibility. Clicking a department opens a filtered team view.
Assessment and action plan: baseline status, priority gaps, actions requiring evidence and links to manage owners and deadlines.
Work queue: pending invitations, incomplete assessment inputs, upcoming sessions, report revisions and unpublished marketplace submissions.
Support and resources: recommended templates, available mentors, AI reporting assistance and recent community discussions.
Coherent example
Asteria Group has 24 invited users, 18 activated and 12 users who completed the current learning path. Display activation as 18/24 = 75 percent and path completion as 12/18 = 67 percent rounded. Do not confuse invited users with the denominator for active learner completion.

11 Assessment and action planning experience
Purpose: help authorised company representatives establish a baseline and turn identified needs into a practical plan. This company assessment is distinct from employee course quizzes and paid certification exams.
Assessment landing page
Display the current assessment name, purpose, owner, status, completion percentage and last updated date.
Show a clear Start or Continue action and an archive of previous assessment periods. Employees do not see this tab by default.
Group questions into manageable sections. Show explanatory help, saved answers, evidence attachments and a final review screen.
The mockup can use a short illustrative diagnostic. Label it Demo Company Assessment. Do not claim that invented questions or scoring reproduce the official Gender Gap Analysis Tool.
Results page
Present strengths, gaps and recommended next actions, with principle and department tags. Show completion of evidence and the assessment period. Any maturity labels or scores must identify the methodology; use a plain needs summary if the methodology is not approved.
Each identified need links to a resource, suggested course, relevant clinic, mentor and Add to Action Plan button. This is the main connection between diagnosis and support.
Action editor and tracking
An action contains title, purpose, principle, owner, department, baseline, target, due date, evidence requirements and review status. Example: Review recruitment shortlisting practices; HR owner; proposed target for the next reporting period; supporting review document.
Create as Draft, request CEO review if configured, then move to In Progress, Evidence Submitted, Complete or Needs Revision.
An employee assigned a contribution can upload a document or update a task without gaining access to unrelated company records.
Record who updated an action and when. Show overdue actions and missing evidence in company and global reporting.
Link assessments to annual reporting through explicit references. A learning activity can support an action, but course completion alone must not mark a company action implemented.
12 Learn and department learning paths
Purpose: give each employee a relevant self-paced pathway, with visible progress and an understandable route to live support. Company CEOs and administrators may also learn through a role-appropriate pathway.
Learning landing page
Top area: My Learning Path, department, progress, next recommended action and points already earned.
Path cards show title, objective, estimated time, lesson count, prerequisite, points and progress. Include filters for principle, topic, status and language.
Provide Continue Learning plus a separate Browse Courses catalogue. A personalised pathway can be generated from department, profile interests and company priorities, with a visible explanation.
Course detail and player
Show overview, learning outcomes, modules, total points and related clinic. The player uses a content canvas, lesson navigation, captions/transcript, resource links, completion controls and knowledge checks. Include video, reading, quiz and reflection activity examples.
Save progress between visits. Completing a lesson updates the employee dashboard, course progress and points ledger.
Use an illustrative 70 percent quiz threshold for demo courses and allow a retry. Label the threshold as a configurable demo rule.
Award points for a unique verified completion event. Reopening a lesson or refreshing a page never awards the same points again.
On path completion, show a short celebration and a direct action to register for the now-eligible clinic.
Path generation scenario
HR users receive inclusive recruitment and workplace practice examples; Procurement receives supplier and procurement examples; Marketing receives inclusive communications. Use proposed course content for the demo and label it accordingly. Official resources can be linked with attribution.
Administrator visibility
Company administrators can see assignment, progress and readiness for their own team. Global administrators manage catalogue entries, pathway rules and aggregate completion. Keep assessment results and quiz performance scoped to the intended role.
13 Resources library
Purpose: make relevant practical guidance easy to discover, preview, download and reuse in company actions and reporting.
Library layout
Use a featured resource area, prominent search and a filter bar. Provide grid and list views. Filters include principle, topic, department, file type, language and access level. Add My Saved Resources and Recently Viewed.
Every resource card includes title, short description, source/publisher, format, language, relevant principle and View or Download action.
The detail page includes an accessible preview, summary, publication metadata, related materials and links to related courses, clinics or action-plan tasks.
Use a document viewer for PDFs and presentations with download controls. Provide a usable alternative when embedded preview is unavailable.
Suggested source-linked examples
Use these official titles as relevant examples, with links to their WEPs pages: WEPs Transparency and Accountability Roadmap; WEPs Gender Action Plan Template; and the Gender-responsive Procurement Toolbox. Label proposed preview art appropriately and respect member-only access. [S3, S4]
For materials requiring an authorised account, show a source link and access note rather than pretending to have downloaded restricted content. Use a sample placeholder document to demonstrate the preview interaction if necessary.
Connected actions
Save to My Resources; Share to a permitted community thread; Attach Reference to a company action; Ask AI About This Resource.
The AI action opens the concierge with the resource context and source reference. It does not automatically expose company information in the public community.
Show a clear empty state when no resources match filters, with Clear Filters and suggested alternatives.
Management
Global administrators upload, tag, version, schedule and archive resources. Metadata includes owner, source URL, file version, language and permitted audience. Changes to an existing resource do not erase historical references in submitted company reports.
14 Clinics webinars and event participation
Purpose: connect self-paced preparation with live learning. Use Clinics as the main user-facing tab; allow event types Webinar and Clinic in filters and details.
Event catalogue and detail
Offer List and Calendar views with topic, principle, format, date, language and eligibility filters. Display the user’s timezone prominently.
Cards show title, facilitator, date/time, duration, format, available places and status: Locked, Eligible, Registered, Waitlisted, Completed or Recording Available.
Detail page includes objectives, agenda, speaker profile, required preparation, earned points, registration action, calendar action and follow-up resources.
Unlock behaviour
For the demo, completing the designated four lessons and path quiz earns 200 qualifying points and unlocks a clinic. The eligibility panel must show both required learning completion and the qualifying milestone. This prevents unrelated community points from bypassing preparation.
Registration does not spend points. Use lifetime qualifying learning points for access, so later mentorship redemption does not remove clinic eligibility. If prerequisites are incomplete, show exactly what remains and link to Continue Learning.
Registration and attendance
Register reserves a place and updates the dashboard and calendar. Full events offer Join Waitlist. Cancellation releases the place; simulate the resulting state.
Show joining instructions only to registered eligible users. A demo join action opens a simulated session page, not a real external meeting.
After the session, record attendance separately from registration. Display materials, recording if permitted, feedback and a practical follow-up action.
Award attendance points once after confirmed participation; registering or repeatedly opening the event does not earn attendance points.
Trainer controls
Trainers see their assigned session roster, participant preparation, materials and follow-up. Global administrators create schedules, assign facilitators, define prerequisites and capacities, and view attendance reporting. Support time changes and cancellation notices.
15 Human mentorship and booking calendar
Purpose: let eligible users find relevant human expertise and redeem earned points for one-to-one support. Include both company-focused advice and employee support within programme rules.
Mentor discovery
Use expert cards with photo, name, expertise, sector, languages, delivery format and next available slot. Filters include topic, language, sector and availability.
A detail screen shows biography, support themes, session length, shared preparation requirements, calendar and points cost.
Recommended matches explain their relevance using the user’s department, company priorities or selected goal. Users can browse other suitable mentors.
Booking flow
Choose expert > choose timezone and available slot > select purpose and optional company action > review shared context > review points cost > confirm booking. The final confirmation shows local date/time, points deducted and cancellation terms.
Proposed demo rule: a 30-minute session costs 500 redeemable personal points. The company administrator can coordinate a company session using their own authorised booking account; do not silently pool employee balances.
If the balance is too low, display the exact shortfall and links to eligible learning activities. Show no misleading Book Now action that fails without explanation.
Reserve the slot and points together. Double-clicking confirmation must not create duplicate bookings or deductions. A slot no longer available prompts selection of another slot.
Show Booked, Completed, Cancelled and No Show states. Proposed demo cancellation rule: cancellation at least 24 hours before the session restores points; later cancellation requires administrator review.
After the session
Show agreed next actions, shared resources and feedback. Mentor private notes remain private. Any company-visible follow-up is clearly labelled and shared only with authorised participants.
Expert calendar
Mentors manage availability, unavailable periods, session limits and booking details. The prototype uses a simulated calendar. Production calendar and conferencing integrations are separately scoped.
16 AI Concierge and community assistant
Purpose: provide useful, profile-aware guidance grounded in approved WEPs information, available resources and authorised user data.
Personal concierge screen
Use a full chat workspace with conversation history, suggested prompts and a contextual side panel. Show the active company and role, relevant sources and suggested next actions. Also provide a small launcher from other portal screens.
Employee prompts: What should I learn next in Procurement; why is this clinic locked; how many points do I need for mentoring?
Company administrator prompts: Which actions need evidence; help draft this year’s progress narrative; which departments need follow-up?
CEO prompts: Summarise our current priorities and decisions; show the progress of our annual report.
Global administrator prompts: Which companies have overdue reports; compare clinic readiness by department; prepare a portfolio summary with source records.
Required answer behaviour
Use the signed-in profile and permissions. Cite the relevant resource or record and explain which period or filter is being used. Provide action buttons such as Open Course, View Report or Go to Booking. Clearly mark generated report text as a draft for review.
The assistant can prepare a draft or prefill a form, but submission, company approval, purchases and disclosure require the user’s explicit final action. It cannot invent missing company indicators or treat an unsupported claim as verified evidence.
Community assistant
Community discussions contain a visibly labelled AI participant answering general WEPs questions from approved knowledge. It can reply in a public thread or a signatory-only thread according to that space’s access rules. It must not bring private company assessments, billing, personal conversations or report drafts into a shared discussion.
Prototype implementation
Seed at least six useful scripted conversations covering employee, company administrator and global reporting needs. Demo responses must match the sample records and demonstrate sources, suggested actions and an unavailable-data response. Do not imply live AI connectivity if the prototype uses scripted responses.

Exam and credential flow

Proposed demo exam: ten questions, 80 percent pass mark, completed by an authorised company representative. Include pass, fail, retry and saved progress states. Final methodology, retake limits and assessment integrity are configurable product decisions.
Credential screen shows company, certification title, issue date, unique reference and download/verification action. Use Sample Certificate on prototype outputs; do not invent official seals or accreditation claims.
Track each offering independently. Never display the same payment as revenue three times. Refund and cancellation states remain visible in the payment record.
The production design uses Stripe payment confirmation to update purchase state, with duplicate-event protection. Expiry, renewal, accreditation and the real payment account require confirmation before launch; the demo should not invent these policies.

 | Two balances and one preparation rule
Lifetime points measure achievements. Redeemable points are the spendable balance. Qualifying learning points are the subset earned from specified preparation. Display them with plain explanations; they need not be three equally prominent dashboard cards.
A designated path has four lessons worth 100 points plus a quiz worth 100. Clinic access requires all designated lessons complete, the quiz passed and 200 qualifying learning points. Unrelated points cannot replace preparation. Booking a clinic consumes no points.
Mentorship requires 500 redeemable points. Example: an employee has 620 lifetime points and 620 available; booking deducts 500, leaving 120 available and 620 lifetime. Clinic eligibility remains intact.
Ledger and experience
Points history shows action, date, amount, source record and resulting balance. Repeated clicks, retries and page reloads do not duplicate awards.
Rewards view shows available opportunities, shortfall and how to earn the missing points. Show both locked and unlocked states.
No points for paying for a certification. No unlimited points for downloads, logins or repeated posts. Corrections use an adjustment record with a reason.
Use personal milestones and badges. Optional community leaderboards can rank contribution or learning only; do not label companies with high engagement points as having the best gender equality outcomes.

23 Executive reporting and impact definitions
Executive dashboard composition
Start with a concise headline and the current reporting period. Use six main cards: approved signatories, active companies, activated users, learning completion, confirmed clinic attendance and annual reporting completion. Below, provide a company progression funnel, reporting status distribution and an attention-required queue.

Attention queue: information requests awaiting response, inactive companies, clinic preparation gaps, overdue actions, report revisions and marketplace reviews.
Insight panel: concise evidence-based observations and suggested follow-up. The global AI assistant can explain the records behind a finding.
Metric definitions
Metric
 | Definition for the prototype
 | Approved signatories
 | Distinct companies currently approved as signatories; do not count employees or applications.

 | Invited users who accepted and accessed their portal.
